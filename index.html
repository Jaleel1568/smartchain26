About the Project

Rethinking Supply Chains Through Responsible AI

Project Coordinator
Prof. Dr.-Ing. João Paulo Javidi da Costa
Hochschule Hamm-Lippstadt, Germany

SmartChain'26 deepens the bilateral partnership between Hochschule Hamm-Lippstadt (HSHL) in Germany and Universidade de Brasília (UnB) in Brazil, funded by the German Academic Exchange Service (DAAD) under the HAW.International programme. The project runs from January to June 2026 and brings together students, doctoral researchers, postdocs, and faculty from both institutions.

At the heart of the project is a pressing global challenge: how can Artificial Intelligence help identify and prevent human rights violations in global supply chains? Using Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Large Language Models (LLMs), mixed international teams develop AI-based methods to detect risks such as forced labour, child labour, and modern slavery supporting compliance with Germany's Supply Chain Due Diligence Act (LkSG, 2023).

Beyond technology, SmartChain'26 integrates ethics, law, and social justice into its research approach. Participants strengthen competencies in Responsible AI, intercultural collaboration, and international research laying the groundwork for a lasting academic partnership, joint degree programmes, and future EU or DAAD research projects.

Research Objectives

Five Core Goals of SmartChain'26

01

AI for Human Rights Due Diligence

Develop NLP and LLM-based methods to detect forced labour, child labour, and modern slavery risks in global supply chain documents supporting compliance with Germany's LkSG Act.

02

Responsible & Explainable AI

Ensure AI systems used in supply chain contexts are fair, transparent, and free from algorithmic bias following the NIST AI-100 framework for trustworthy AI.

03

Interdisciplinary Teaching

Co-develop a hybrid course "AI and Social Justice in Global Supply Chains" taught jointly by HSHL and UnB, covering machine learning, ethics, and UN human rights frameworks.

04

Joint Research & Publication

Produce a comparative case study or pilot study for submission to an international conference or journal, and develop a follow-up EU or DAAD multi-year research proposal.

05

Knowledge Transfer

Host a closing event with NGOs, industry, and government partners, and produce a bilingual policy brief making project findings accessible to a broader public audience.

Workshop Activities

International Knowledge Exchange

WS 1 NLP · ML May 2026 · Brasília, Brazil

Applying Machine Learning to Detect Modern Slavery in Supply Chains

Hernany Silveira Rocha & Jaqueline Duarte presented an NLP-based framework using LLMs, clustering, and text classification to identify forced labour cases in supply chain documents. The framework covers data collection, GPT-assisted analysis, sentence extraction, vectorization, and model training.

WS 2 RAI · XAI May 2026 · Brasília, Brazil

Responsible & Explainable AI in Supply Chain Contexts

Jaqueline D. Duarte presented the NIST AI-100 framework covering trustworthy AI principles — validity, safety, fairness, privacy, and explainability — alongside AI lifecycle management and compliance requirements for AI systems deployed in high-stakes supply chain decisions.

WS 3 NLP · Legal May 2026 · Brasília, Brazil

Multilingual Named Entity Recognition for Legal Documents

Ana Sofia Schweizer Silvestre (UnB) demonstrated NLP techniques for extracting named entities from multilingual legal texts a key capability for automating the analysis of supplier contracts, audit reports, and compliance documents across different languages.

WS 4 Systems May 2026 · Brasília, Brazil

Smart Systems & Supply Chain Technology - HSHL Perspective

Kevin Herman Muraro Gularte (HSHL) contributed the German institutional perspective on smart systems research, presenting ongoing work at Hochschule Hamm-Lippstadt and exploring synergies with the Brazilian team's research focus areas.

WS 5 Closing Planned · Lippstadt, Germany

Second Workshop & Closing Symposium - HSHL, Germany

The final workshop will be hosted at Hochschule Hamm-Lippstadt. It will present all project outputs - AI prototypes, ethical guidelines, teaching modules - and include feedback from external stakeholders such as NGOs, industry partners, and government representatives.
